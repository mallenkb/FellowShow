1

Escape should cancel, not blank the audience screen.

Error preventionCriticalInferred

Evidence. Clear shows an Esc shortcut beside Show on Live. Source lines 554 and 728–735 route Escape outside text fields directly to clearLive().

Problem. An ordinary dismissal key is also an immediate output-changing command. The prototype labels cleared content OFF AIR, which does not establish whether physical outputs or a stream have stopped.

Why it matters. An operator trying to dismiss a selection could remove the projected content during a service.

Recommendation. Reserve Escape for closing or cancelling. Give blackout a dedicated configurable shortcut and a clearly named control, plus Restore previous. Keep content-cleared and output-disconnected states distinct.

Acceptance. A dismissal never changes Program. Blackout is deliberate and reversible. Validate focus and modal precedence before shipping.

Confidence Medium; prototype onlyScope Proposal, not production app
2

Opening a view must not imply starting or ending a sermon.

System status feedbackCriticalInferred

Evidence. Source lines 594–602 derive Start sermon / End sermon and the sermon status from the selected view. Open transcript calls that same setView() at line 620; the clock increments independently at the end of the script.

Problem. The display of session state is coupled to navigation. Browsing a transcript looks like starting a sermon; returning to songs looks like ending it.

Why it matters. Operators need to trust whether recording, transcription and sermon capture are actually running while they prepare other content.

Recommendation. Keep session lifecycle separate from workspace selection. Start and End must be explicit session actions; Open transcript must only navigate. Preserve the running timer and listening/error status in every view.

Acceptance. Switching among Song, Sermon and Slides changes neither session state nor elapsed time. Start/End labels reflect the session, not the visible panel.

Confidence Medium; prototype onlyScope Proposal, not production app
3

Planned order, prepared content and live content need separate states.

Consistency and navigationMediumInferred

Evidence. The capture shows Benediction in Preview while Notices remains NEXT and When I Survey is LIVE. Source advanceOrder() marks every earlier entry done; clearLive() and logo() do not update the order strip.

Problem. The order strip combines planned progress with live output. Skipping ahead implies earlier items were completed, and clearing Program can leave a LIVE order badge behind.

Why it matters. The operator can mistake the next planned item for the next thing that Enter will actually send.

Recommendation. Keep independent labels for Now live, Prepared and Next in plan. Mark completion explicitly or by an agreed rule, not merely list position. Reconcile live badges on clear, logo and out-of-plan cues.

Acceptance. Skip, repeat, clear and temporary scripture cues leave truthful order labels. Show the prepared item title beside the take action.

Confidence Medium; prototype onlyScope Proposal, not production app
4

Display health and Auto-take need an explicit operating contract.

System status feedbackMediumNeeds verification

Evidence. The right column shows green output switches and an Auto-take switch. In the supplied HTML these are decorative spans/divs, and take() sets ON AIR after changing local markup (lines 449–467 and 549–552).

Risk. The proposal shows enabled intent, not successful delivery. It does not define which destination a take affects or what triggers Auto-take.

Why verify. A healthy local monitor does not prove that the audience screen or stream received the frame.

Recommendation. Label each destination with routing plus Connected, Sending or Failed state. Separate enabled from acknowledged delivery. Keep Auto-take off by default and define its source, destination, delay and cancellation before offering it.

Acceptance. A disconnected output cannot appear healthy. Failed delivery offers retry while preserving the last good frame. Automation state stays visible.

Confidence Medium; prototype onlyScope Proposal, not production app
5

Preserve the existing search behaviour when adopting this layout.

Flexibility and efficiencyMediumNeeds verification

Evidence. The Scripture field retains “Type: J → John 3:16”. The prototype quickGo() uses a reference regex and book matching; song search is a decorative div and version chips are spans (lines 677–699).

Risk. This is a layout sketch, not evidence of phrase search, typo tolerance, cross-translation matching or remembered song filters.

Why verify. Those are already explicit operator requirements and are more valuable during a live service than a second search entry point.

Recommendation. Keep natural-language and reference search in the existing field, without renaming it or adding a tab. Match across translations but open the reference in the selected version. Retain song-source filters across restarts. Treat global search as an optional shortcut into the same engine.

Acceptance. With NIV selected, a KJV phrase finds the NIV verse. Misspellings and colonless references work. EasyWorship selection persists. Set and measure a warm-search latency budget on real libraries.

Confidence Medium; prototype onlyScope Proposal, not production app
6

Keep sermon tools reachable without sacrificing the song workspace.

Simplicity and information architectureMediumVisible

Evidence. The capture has a useful one-line transcript and one detected reference. The source retains Detected, Related, Live notes and Summary inside the hidden Sermon pane (lines 410–429).

Problem. The compact view exposes only one cue and provides no visible backlog or freshness indication. The four sermon tools require leaving the current workspace.

Why it matters. A new scripture cue or transcription failure can be missed while the operator works through song parts.

Recommendation. Preserve the four existing tabs inside Sermon. Add a compact new-cue count and freshness/error state to the strip, with an optional pinned or expandable tray that leaves song tiles in place. Do not add another top-level mode.

Acceptance. The operator can inspect a recent cue, prepare it and return to the same song part and scroll position without changing the sermon session.

Confidence High for visible structureScope Proposal, not production app
7

Use compact controls without hiding names or scroll affordances.

Recognition rather than recallMediumVisible

Evidence. Completed order chips are heavily faded, the book grid truncates “Song of Sol...”, and display routing truncates “HDMI 1 · gene...”. Source hides the order-strip scrollbar and fits a fixed 1440-pixel canvas by scaling.

Problem. Low-emphasis items can look disabled; clipped labels and invisible overflow make information harder to scan at smaller operator-window sizes.

Why it matters. During a service, recovering a previous item or checking the target output should not depend on hovering or discovering horizontal scrolling.

Recommendation. Keep completed items readable with an explicit completed marker. Give routing labels room, use recognised book abbreviations with full names on focus, and reveal strip overflow with arrows or an edge cue. Reflow or resize columns rather than shrinking the whole interface.

Acceptance. At the minimum supported desktop width, live controls remain readable, route names are available without pointer-only hover, and hidden order items are discoverable. Keyboard, contrast and zoom checks remain to be run.

Confidence High for visible structureScope Proposal, not production app
8

Keep the library, work area and output monitors distinct.

Real world and mental modelWorking wellVisible

Evidence. The captured layout gives song parts large tiles in the centre, keeps Scripture available on the left, and places Preview, Show on Live and Program together on the right.

Why it works. It supports preparing a different content type while the current song remains live. Explicit LIVE badges help distinguish the audience output from work in progress.

What to preserve. Keep those three responsibilities separate. Retain the prominent take button and full lyric context. Do not force the library selection to follow Program or automatically send a newly opened item live.

Acceptance. An operator can browse scripture while a song stays on Program, then deliberately take the prepared verse.

Success

The right content reaches the intended output.

Assumed success measure: no unintended clear or take, no false session status, and fast recovery. These outcomes have not been measured.

Keep

Distinct preparation and output regions.

Retain large song-part tiles, the service strip and compact transcript. Keep Detected, Related, Live notes and Summary within Sermon.

Fix first

Navigation must not control sessions.

Opening a transcript is not starting a sermon. Dismissing a panel is not blanking Program. Address observations 1 and 2 first.

Then improve

Make every state truthful.

Separate Prepared from Next in plan; show real destination health and preserve existing search/filter behaviour. Observations 3–5.

Avoid

A redesign that removes working capabilities.

Do not rename the Scripture field, add a context-search tab, replace Media with Overlays without covering canvas workflows, or infer production readiness from this mockup.

03 · Decision memo

Refine the interaction contract before moving panels.

Adopt the layout direction, not the prototype behaviour wholesale. Keep the existing app’s proven search, media and session capabilities when moving them into this structure.
